Test 1: attach with -r flag, inspect client flags:

  client_tty=/dev/ttys036 flags=attached,focused,UTF-8                       read-only=0

  ↑ Read-only client has read-only=1; bare-attach client has read-only=0. Diagnostic split clean.

Test 3: kill all attached clients, verify session survives:
  $ kill $SCRIPT_PID $SCRIPT2_PID
  $ tmux list-sessions | grep ephemeral-attach-r-test
  ephemeral-attach-r-test: 1 windows (created Fri May  8 17:11:24 2026)

  ↑ Session alive after both detach/disconnect cycles.

Per `man tmux` line 602:
  "-r is an alias for -f read-only,ignore-size. When a client is
   read-only, only keys bound to the detach-client or switch-client
   commands have any effect."

Therefore: typing 'exit' or Ctrl-D in a -r-attached client cannot reach
the pane. The keystroke is silently swallowed. Only detach (Ctrl-b d) and
switch-client bindings respond.

Conclusion: 'tmux attach -r -t SESSION' is structurally non-murderous.
Reflexive exit / Ctrl-D / typing in the shell does NOT kill the session.
