Phone Mode — the new screen

This is a preview of the design. Tap UPDATE in the app to get it on your phone.

The wallpapers here are stand-ins so you can see how it holds up on both a dark and a light background. On your phone it's your wallpaper behind it.

The clock and date

  • Hours stacked over minutes, oversized and pushed to the left edge. The off-center placement is what keeps it from looking like stock Android.
  • It reads your wallpaper and adapts. Light wallpaper, it switches to dark lettering on its own — that's the second picture above. You don't set anything.
  • It stays readable over a photo. Every letter gets a soft outline, so it doesn't disappear into a busy background the way plain white text does.
  • Uses the app's own typeface rather than the generic Android one, and the digits are locked to equal width so the clock doesn't twitch sideways when the minute changes.
  • Tap the time to open your clock app. Tap the date to open your calendar on today.
  • The date now shows the whole bit — weekday, month name, day, year, and underneath the month/day numbers plus the week of the year.

The bottom tray

  • A fixed dock along the bottom for your special apps — the ones you always want in thumb reach, like camera, messages and maps.
  • It starts off with sensible picks. To add one: swipe up for the full list, press and hold any app, choose "Add to bottom tray". To remove one: press and hold it in the tray.
  • Unlike the main grid, the tray never re-sorts itself — what you put there stays put.

Your background

  • It's your real system wallpaper — whatever you've set on your phone shows through.
  • Press and hold anywhere empty on the screen to jump straight to the wallpaper picker and change it, exactly like a normal Android home screen.

Your apps

  • Your pinned favorites come first, in the order you pinned them — they're never dropped or reshuffled.
  • After those, it fills the remaining spots with what you're most likely to open right now, which shifts by time of day.
  • Press and hold an icon for a menu — Move, add or remove from favorites, add to the bottom tray, or app info. Same menu you get in the full app list.
  • To rearrange: hold an icon, choose Move, then tap the spot you want it to go. The icon you're moving dims so you can see which one is in flight. Tap empty space to cancel. Once you arrange the screen by hand it stays that way.
  • The grid is five across now, so it holds ten apps.
  • Swipe up for the full app list with search. That list is now just your apps and the search box — the duplicate favorites row at the top is gone.

Switching modes

  • The button now shows where it takes you — a grid when you're in Homestead (tap for the phone screen), a little house when you're in phone mode. It flips over like a coin when you tap it.
  • The switch itself is a circle that opens and closes from the button under your thumb. Going to phone mode, Homestead shrinks away into the button and uncovers your wallpaper. Coming back, it grows out of the button and covers it again.
  • It runs in about a third of a second — same speed as before, just no longer an instant hard cut.
To see it for real: open Homestead and tap UPDATE, then tap the grid button on the right edge. There's a short checklist at the test page if you want to run through it.
